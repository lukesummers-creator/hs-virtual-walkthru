# SMOKE/PET ODOR

We understand your goal is to remedy the lingering odor and stains resulting from 19 years of live-in smoking. The odor is known to cling to porous surfaces inside the house, including gaps, seams and underlayment of flooring.

Our process consists of 2 essential services to mitigate the odor:

(1) Re-paint all hard surfaces (walls, trim, doors, cabinets, vanities, etc.) and prime with an odor and stain repellent primer.

(2) Remove carpets and LVP, prime subfloors and existing laminate, and install new flooring.

We also strongly suggest replacing ceiling fans as the odor is often trapped in the natural buildup on fan blades and within the housing.

# CABINET PAINTING (long)

Prep and paint all kitchen cabinet doors, drawer fronts, face frames, exposed cabinet sides, toe kicks, and both bathroom vanities. We do not paint interior of cabinets or vanities. Existing hardware will be reinstalled; new hardware can be installed if pre-purchased by client.

Application Method & Finish Level

Cabinets and vanities will be brush and roll finished; no spraying is included in this quote.
Surfaces will be cleaned, de-glossed, and scuff-sanded, then receive 1 coat Sherwin-Williams Extreme Bond Primer and 2 finish coats Sherwin-Williams Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel.
This system provides a durable, professional finish with visible oak grain and subtle roller/brush texture typical of an on-site brush-and-roll application.

Wood Grain & Color Selection

Existing cabinets and vanities are oak. This quote does not include grain filling; natural wood grain and texture will remain visible after painting.
Dark colors may highlight the oak grain pattern. For a smoother, more “factory-like” look with reduced grain telegraphing, a separate grain-fill and spray upgrade can be quoted at a higher price.

Sprayed and grain-filled finish options are available as premium upgrades and can be priced separately if desired.

## When to use what
- MSG 2 = default.
- MSG 1 = when the proposal includes option items/toggles that the client can opt in/out.
- MSG 3 = when we need to inject value around a fully managed service AND/OR when we intentionally went “all in” on scope knowing we’ll scale it back.
  - If we’re just scaling back (without needing the “fully managed” framing), do not include the fully managed paragraph.

## Required timeline line (always include)
Always include a timeline estimate. When less confident, widen the range and add a qualifier like: “depending on final scope” and/or reference repairs and dry times.
